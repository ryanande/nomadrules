# Business Concept

## Problem

Full-time RVers, digital nomads, and domicile-state residents face a compounding legal blind spot:

1. They research domicile law *once* — when they first establish residency.
2. Laws change constantly across insurance, tax, DMV, and voting domains.
3. No service delivers plain-English, personalized alerts when those changes affect them.
4. The consequences of missing a change can be significant — lapsed coverage, surprise tax liability, invalid registration, or disenfranchisement.

## Opportunity

~1.1M full-time RVers in the US (growing ~10% YoY), plus millions of:
- Digital nomads and remote workers with out-of-state domicile
- Snowbirds (part-year residents of FL, TX, AZ)
- Retirees who relocated domicile for tax purposes
- RV-adjacent professionals: attorneys, tax preparers, mail forwarding services, RV parks

None of these groups are being served a timely, automated, personalized feed of "here is what changed in your domicile state this month and why it matters to you."

## Value proposition

> **NomadRules watches the laws so you don't have to.**

For subscribers: peace of mind that nothing important slipped by. For businesses (attorneys, tax preparers, mail forwarding services): a white-labeled feed they can offer clients as a value-add.

## Target personas

### Primary — the full-time RVer

- Lives 100% on the road, domiciled in SD, TX, or FL
- Works remotely or is retired
- Technically comfortable (smartphones, apps, email) but not a lawyer
- Made domicile decision 1–5 years ago and hasn't revisited it
- **Pain:** unaware of what's changed since they set everything up
- **Willingness to pay:** $9–$19/month for peace of mind

### Secondary — the digital nomad entrepreneur

- Remote worker or online business owner
- Domiciled for tax optimization reasons
- Likely has an LLC; cares about nexus, pass-through income rules
- **Pain:** tax law and business registration rules change; accountant is expensive for ongoing monitoring
- **Willingness to pay:** $19–$49/month; may expense it as a business cost

### B2B — RV-adjacent professionals

- RV-friendly attorneys, domicile tax preparers, mail forwarding services (Escapees, America's Mailbox, Traveling Mailbox)
- RV parks that offer domicile assistance services
- **Pain:** clients call them every time something changes; they need to be proactive
- **Willingness to pay:** $49–$199/month for white-labeled API or team access

## Competitive landscape

| Competitor | What they do | Gap |
|------------|-------------|------|
| Escapees RV Club | Community, mail forwarding, some domicile guidance | Static content, not monitored |
| RVer Insurance Exchange | Insurance brokerage | No regulatory monitoring |
| State government websites | Primary source | No aggregation, no alerts, no plain English |
| Generic legal alert services (Westlaw, etc.) | Law firm tooling | Not RV-specific, expensive, not consumer-friendly |

**No one is doing this.** The gap is real.

## Business model summary

See [Subscription Model](./05-subscription-model.md) for full detail.

- **Primary revenue:** recurring SaaS subscriptions (3 tiers)
- **Secondary revenue:** affiliate partnerships with RV attorneys, mail forwarding services, insurance brokers
- **Tertiary revenue:** B2B API/white-label licensing

## Unfair advantages

1. **Domain authority** — founders are full-time RVers; authentic community credibility
2. **Community distribution** — direct access to Facebook groups, Escapees forums, iRV2, YouTube RV channels
3. **Self-managing by design** — AI-powered pipeline minimizes ongoing operational cost
4. **Compounding moat** — the law change archive becomes more valuable over time; historical diffs are hard to replicate
